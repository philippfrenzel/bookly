# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Hobby authors and writing-course students who develop the plot of a book. They plan at a desktop browser, often between writing sessions or during a course, and want to see whether their story holds together. Many are new to structured plotting; the tool must be easy to start with and encouraging, never intimidating.

## Product Purpose

Bookly gives an author a bird's-eye view of a book's storylines so the red thread stays visible ("Story ergibt Sinn"). Authors lay out main and side strands on a story timeline, connect them with typed connections, mark each strand's status, and get hints when time or characters contradict each other. Success: the author can see at a glance how the story flows and where it does not yet make sense.

## Positioning

Two things together, equally weighted:
- **Bird's-eye view with hints, never corrections.** The map shows the whole story at once, and Bookly points out time and character contradictions but never changes the author's data. The author decides (BR-002).
- **Structure only, no prose.** Bookly is deliberately not a writing program. Writing out the details of a storyline is out of scope; there are storylines, sections, a story timeline, characters, connections and notes, nothing more.

## Operating Context

- One author per account; register, login and logout only.
- A book has an ordered story timeline of named time points (for example "Kindheit", "Tag 1", "3 Jahre später"), not calendar dates.
- Edits save directly; there is no save button for field edits. Creating a storyline or section uses „Anlegen“; the connection dialog confirms with „Speichern“.
- UI copy is German.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Storylines carry a title, a short description, characters, a time range, an optional note, a kind (main or side) and a colour.
- Main and side strands differ by line thickness (main thicker), colour and position (mains in the centre, sides at the edge). The kind can be changed at any time.
- Status: „in Bearbeitung“ (start status), „abgeschlossen“, „bewusst offen“, each shown by colour and a symbol (✎, ✓, ○).
- Main strands can be divided into sections with their own time ranges; side strands cannot.
- Scenes are places: a name, an optional image and any number of notes. A scene can be assigned to several sections; each assignment carries a short scene description (place and atmosphere, never prose). The latest stored description is offered when assigning again; the AI drafts one from image and notes only on click.
- Connection types: „Figur wechselt“, „Zeitsprung“, „Handlungsstränge vereinigen sich“, „Rückblende“, plus free text.
- Hints for time contradictions and character overlaps appear immediately while creating a connection and can be acknowledged. They never block saving.
- Filters: main strands only, open strands only, time range, characters. Zoom and pan; notes can be shown on the map.
- No upper limit on storylines; smooth at 200 storylines, desktop browsers only.
- Deleting a connected storyline warns and removes its connections.

## Brand Commitments

Only the name "Bookly" is set. Logo, voice and look are open.

## Evidence on Hand

No testimonials, users, screenshots or marketing assets exist. Do not invent any. The demo book "Der Weg nach Norden" is test data only.

## Product Principles

1. **The author decides.** Bookly shows and points out; it never rewrites, merges or "fixes" the story.
2. **The whole story at a glance.** The map is the product; panels and forms serve it.
3. **Encourage, don't intimidate.** First steps are obvious, empty states guide, and hints read as help, not errors.
4. **Structure, not prose.** No feature pulls the author into writing out scenes.
5. **No ceiling.** The tool stays readable and usable as the number of strands grows.
